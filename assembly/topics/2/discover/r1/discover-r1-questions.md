**1 Think of a time you compared a system before and after a change. How did you know you were looking at the same software, and how did you know which version you were looking at?**
*Describe the system and the change. What identified the software in your telemetry, and what marked the change: a version your team set, a build or image, a deployment time, or something else? Where did that break down, and what happened because of it?*

**2 Where does energy or carbon data in your systems come from something other than the software itself, such as a node agent, a platform or a provider?**
*Could that data be tied back to the software and the release it belonged to? If not, what did you do with it? Should a number that cannot be tied to a release still count when comparing two releases, and what would it need to say about itself?*

**3 When you judge whether a system did "the same work" before and after a change, what work do you count?**
*Name the unit for a system you know, such as requests, transactions, jobs or users. Where did the count come from: telemetry you already had, something you had to add, or somewhere else? How would someone reading the number know what kind of work was counted?*

**4 How do you tell a measured value from a calculated, estimated or forecast one?**
*Give an example of each from tools or systems you know, or say which you have never seen. Where does the line between calculated and estimated fall for you? The SCI recognizes two quantification methods, measurement and calculation: where does each of the four belong?*

**5 What would you need to know about how two numbers were produced before you trusted they were produced the same way?**
*Is the name and version of the tool or model enough, or do you also need its input data, such as which carbon intensity source or power model it used? Describe a time a change in method or input moved a number when the software itself had not changed.*

**6 Should a value carry an indication of how confident its producer is in it?**
*If yes, what would you do differently with a low-confidence value, and who could realistically produce that indication today? If no, what would a confidence indicator cost or mislead?*
